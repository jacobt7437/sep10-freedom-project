# Entry 2: Further Researching my Topic
##### 11/19/26

### Additional Research

As I mentioned in my last entry, I planned to do additional research on my topic, medical records. As such, I researched both software and hardware technologies involved in creating and storing these records. Through my searches, I learned about four technologies; ultrasounds, MRI machines, MyChart, and applications of AI for the subject.

In researching ultrasounds, I learned their various applications and how the procedure is performed. First, gel is applied to the patient, after which a handheld transducer sends sound waves into the patient's body, creating an image using the ones that bounce back. I learned this information from these articles: [Ultrasound - Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/ultrasound/about/pac-20395177) and [The History of Ultrasound](https://www.bmus.org/for-patients/history-of-ultrasound/).

Furthermore, in researching MRI machines, I learned that the acronym MRI stands for magnetic resonance imaging, and that the machine uses magnetic fields and radio waves to create images of a patient's organs for later examination. I learned this information from these articles: [The History of the MRI: The Development of Medical Resonance Imaging](https://mccollege.edu/aas-in-magnetic-resonance-imaging-mri-technology/about-the-mri-technology-career/the-history-of-the-mri-development-of-medical-resonance-imaging/) and [MRI](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/mri/about/pac-20384768).

Moreover, in researching MyChart, I learned that it is a application used by hospitals to store patients' medical information and make it easily accessible to them. I learned this information from these articles: [MyChart Feature Guide - Children's Health](https://www.childrens.com/patient-families/parent-resources/mychart-guide), [Manage your health with MyChart 24/7](https://www.nychealthandhospitals.org/mychart/), and [MyChart—A New Mode of Care Delivery: 2005 Personal Health Link Research Report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3057730/).

Finally, in researching applications of AI in storing and recording medical records, I learned that LLMs, or large language models, record patient speech and translate it onto a digital record to allow for doctors to have more time to tend to patients. I learned this information from these articles: [AI Can Outperform Humans in Writing Medical Summaries](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-can-outperform-humans-writing-medical-summaries) and [Artificial Intelligence and Healthcare: A Journey through History, Present Innovations, and Future Possibilities](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11122160/#sec4-life-14-00557).

I organized my research by the time these technologies were invented, in this order:
1. Ultrasound (1950s)
2. MRI (1997)
3. MyChart (2002)
4. AI technology in medical records (2020)
