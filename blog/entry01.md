# Entry 1: Choosing a Topic (Medical Records)
##### 10/19/26

## Why Medical Records?

For a while, I had a bleak outlook on my future. I thought there would be no sustainable job I would enjoy. However, I'm not sure why, but I started thinking about working in cybersecurity, and asked my parents about it. My mom told me that healthcare institutions require skilled cybersecurity workers to protect medical records, as they contain patients' personal information and, as such, are often targeted by hackers. A job in that field sounded appealing to me, so I figured I could use this project as an opportunity to research how these records used to and currently work, how technology has been implemented in their storage and protection, and how the system could be improved upon in the future. On top of that, not many people know about how technology has impacted medical records, and my website would be an effective way to teach them about the topic as well as myself.

## Research

Some articles I used to explore the history of medical records and how technology is used to store them were [Electronic Health Records: Then, Now, and in the Future](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5171496/), [Electronic Health Records Explained](https://www.iso.org/healthcare/electronic-health-records), and [The Complete History of Electronic Health Records](https://patagoniahealth.com/blog/the-history-of-electronic-health-records), all of which provided some insight on how technology used to store patients' medical records has become more advanced over time. It was more difficult to find out how they were protected, but I found one article about the flaws with these technological systems in that regard: [Electronic Health Records Security: Patient Data, HIPAA, and the Threats Healthcare Cannot Afford to Ignore](https://www.baculasystems.com/blog/electronic-health-record-security/). Through this article, I learned what flaws in these systems make data breaches more likely. Perhaps **future technology** would be able to prevent these breaches.

## Engineering Design Process

I have completed step 1 of the Engineering Design Process: defining the problem. **Both myself and the general populace have little knowledge on the technology used in medical records**, and I would like to rectify that. Now I am in step 2: researching the problem. I have found great sources already, but I would need to find additional ones and review what I already have to be informed enough to make a website on the topic.

## Skills

From my research, I learned two valuable skills:

#### How to Google

My searches brought up large amounts of results, and I had neither the time nor the patience to scour them all. First I looked at the titles to see if they would be of interest to me, and if they were, I quickly skimmed the articles to see if they were what I was looking for. This way, I was able to quickly find articles to use for research. I believe repeating this process when researching other topics will greatly expedite the process.

#### How to Read Articles Efficiently

The articles I selected were all ridiculously long. They obviously had what I was looking for, but I didn't need _all_ of the information they contained. However, they all had a table of contents and subsections. By making use of those, I was able to find the parts of the articles I cared about instead of spending unnecessary amounts of time reading each one. Using this process will also help to expedite any research I do in the future.

## Next Steps

The next step for me in this project would be to continue my research. After that, I will move on to step 3 of the Engineering Design Process: brainstorming possible solutions. In this context, that would mean to have some loose ideas of what my website will look like and how the information on it will be organized.
